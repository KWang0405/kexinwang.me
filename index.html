About

I’m a second-year PhD student in Civil Engineering at USC, advised by Prof. Ruolin Li in the SIAS Lab. I’m also pursuing an MS in Computer Science.

I study how independent agents make decisions together. On a crowded highway, a lane change that saves one driver time can slow down everyone behind them. I use game theory and multi-agent learning to understand these effects and design ways for vehicles to coordinate.

Research

I study how independent agents can cooperate when each sees only part of the system and controls only part of the outcome. I use traffic as a testbed to ask how drivers respond to one another, how driving models negotiate, and how much authority a coordinator needs.

Mixed Autonomy

One driver’s lane change can delay everyone behind them. I model how human drivers respond to strategic autonomous vehicles at bottlenecks. In MAPLE, we turn predicted congestion into lane-change incentives that account for those delays.

Driving VLAs

What happens when two driving models both yield? I’m studying how independently trained vision-language-action models can coordinate through role cues they already understand. I test these interactions in closed-loop CARLA simulations with both models’ weights frozen.

Bounded Authority

In Bounded Mediation, a language model can adjust incentives or narrow the available actions, while the vehicle’s controller chooses the maneuver. My ongoing work on BOND asks when one more inference step is worth spending a limited compute budget.
